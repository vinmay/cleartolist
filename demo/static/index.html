How we handle photos
  • We use your photos only to check them for recalls. Nothing else.
  • Before the check, we remove hidden details from them, like where the photo was taken.
  • We delete your photos as soon as the check is done. If it's busy, they're deleted within a few minutes anyway.
  • We never sell or share your photos, and we don't keep a copy.
  • No cookies and no tracking. We only count things like how many checks were done each day, without knowing who did them.

How it works

What it checks

Your photos are checked against the official U.S. list of product recalls from the Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC), going back to 2015 and kept up to date.

It covers secondhand products of every kind, with children's products first: cribs, bassinets, strollers, high chairs and toys, plus heaters, chargers, furniture, tools and more. Car seats aren't included: they're recalled through NHTSA.

How to get the best result

A photo of the label is the most reliable way to confirm a recall. A photo of the item alone can miss recalls.

Look for a sticker or tag with a model number, barcode or date code: under the seat, on the frame or on the base. Buying online? A screenshot of the listing works, and you can ask the seller for a photo of the label.

What a result means

"Recalled" means the model number or barcode matches a recall. "Check the label" means it looks like a recalled product. No match isn't proof an item is safe.
