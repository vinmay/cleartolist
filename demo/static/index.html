How we handle photos

Your photos are checked to be real JPEG, PNG or WebP images, then re-encoded, which removes all metadata, including GPS location. They're used for this one scan by a self-hosted GPU worker running open models, kept in memory only, and deleted as soon as the scan finishes. If the scanner is busy, they're deleted anyway after eight minutes at most. The text result is kept for ten minutes so this page can show it. We keep anonymous counts of scans, never photos, filenames or addresses.

How it works

What it checks

Your photos are checked against U.S. CPSC recalls since 2015, updated nightly.

Car seats aren't included: they're recalled through NHTSA.

How to get the best result

A photo of the label is the most reliable way to confirm a recall. A photo of the item alone can miss recalls.

Look for a sticker or tag with a model number, barcode or date code: under the seat, on the frame or on the base.

What a result means

"Recalled" means the model number or barcode matches a recall. "Check the label" means it looks like a recalled product. No match isn't proof an item is safe.
