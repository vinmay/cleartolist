How we measured this

We tested the scanner on recall photos it had never seen. Each test photo was a CPSC recall photo, cropped, rotated, dimmed and blurred to look like a phone shot, and that photo and every copy of it were hidden from the scanner, so it could only match through other photos of the same recall. For lookalikes, we used licensed photos of ordinary products in the same categories. The rules were chosen on a separate set of recalls before the test was run once.

From an item photo alone, the scanner shows the right recall about a third of the time. A label photo is what makes a verdict reliable: "Recalled" needs a model number or UPC that matches a recall, and it was right 4 times out of 5 across all test photos, including label close-ups. Other categories get more "Check the label" prompts on ordinary products (sports gear, kitchen goods and tools most often).

The full numbers, every post-test change and its disclosure, and the limits (simulated phone photos, not real thrift-store photos) are in the evaluation write-up.
